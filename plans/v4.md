## Not doing (the evidence says no)

- **Repo-wide autocompact at a fixed threshold.** It saves 3–12% and hurts short sessions. D4 replaces it with Laya's boundary judgement.
- **A Workflow-based wave engine.** The gain is 4–6% IE and 2–5 min, questions would arrive late, and a skill the model triggers itself may not count as opt-in. Later, as a user-typed `/polaris-wave`.
- **Letting Laya enforce the ban.** It is a probabilistic model, near chance zero-shot. It only *chooses*; deterministic layers *veto*.
- **Narrowing model-guard's matcher.** You chose per call, and a Fable session burns quota through Read and Grep too.
- **Raising SOLO's ceiling or multi-task SOLO in one context.** A second task in carried context costs about 9× a fresh one.
- **Switching builders from Bash reads to Read.** Refuted: they are at parity.
- **The broad 55 KB prose trim.** Under 1% of spend. Only contradictions and obsolete sections are cut.
- **SURFACES and `test_select` for the kit.** 59% of commits touch shared modules.
- **Running qa keys in parallel.** doctor reads the zip that build rewrites.
- **Allowing `git checkout -- <path>`.** F-3 closes the `restore .` hole instead.
- **Another multi-agent audit before shipping.**

## Risks and their guards

- **A model-guard misread.** Every fail-open path is kept; the verdict must match across 5 tail shapes; the `mg_denied` parity drill stays; `availableModels` is an independent veto.
- **The ownership prefilter skips a rule.** Any path/ask/content match, or a `feat/*` branch, still reaches `cmd_guard`; the verdicts in guard-denies stay identical.
- **The drift rewrite changes a verdict.** Findings stay byte-identical on a fixture, and old and new drift agree on the live board before merge.
